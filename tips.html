Tips & strategy

Three stars means matching the shortest solution. These habits will get you there more often, and they make the hardest boards feel calm instead of frustrating.

1. Read the board before the first swipe

The most common mistake is to start swiping straight away. Take a few seconds first. Look for the tiles that are hardest to reach: dead ends, single tiles tucked into a corner, narrow side pockets. Those squares usually decide the whole route, because there are only one or two ways the ball can ever stop next to them.

2. Think about where a move ends, not where it goes

The ball always slides until something stops it, so the only places you can "stand" are tiles next to a wall, a stopper or another blocker. Experienced players mentally mark these resting points and plan the route as a chain of them. If a tile can only be painted by passing over it, ask yourself which resting point lets you pass over it.

3. Save dead ends for the right moment

A dead-end corridor is easy to paint: roll in, roll back out. But leaving it often costs a move. Try to enter a dead end when the move out also paints something new. A route that never repeats tiles is usually close to the shortest one.

4. Work backwards from the last tile

When a board feels impossible, choose the tile that will probably be painted last and ask how the ball could arrive there. Then ask where it was the move before that. Two or three steps backwards are often enough to reveal the whole plan.

5. Use undo as an experiment, not a crutch

Undo is limited, so use it with purpose. Make a move, look at what it opened or closed, and step back if it went wrong. If you are out of undos, restarting the level gives them all back, and a fresh start often shows the board in a new light.

Tips for special tiles

Stoppers and arrows

Stoppers are your friends in long, open corridors: they give you a place to stand where there is no wall. Arrows always turn the ball the same way, so treat them as fixed corners that you can approach from any side.

Portals

Before moving, trace each portal pair with your eyes. Remember that the ball keeps its direction after teleporting, so the side you enter from decides which way you leave.

Cracked ice

Ice can be crossed only once, so it almost always belongs late in the route or on a single straight pass. The classic trap is breaking an ice tile early and cutting off the only path to a corner.

Switches and barriers

Count how many times you will touch the switch. Each touch flips every barrier, so an even number of touches leaves them where they started. Pass through an open barrier before you touch the switch that closes it.

Mirrors and geysers

Draw the bounce in your head: a mirror turns the ball by a right angle, and a geyser skips exactly three tiles. Both can reach places that look sealed off by walls.

Clock dials

Dials remember. Every visit turns the hand a quarter, so the order of visits matters more than anywhere else in the game. Write the sequence down in your head: first visit up, second visit right, and so on.

Wind gates

Note which way the wind blows through each gate before planning. A gate you approach against the wind is simply a wall, which can also be useful: it is one more place where the ball can stop.

Keeping it fun

Paintline is meant to be calm. There is no timer in the story levels, and two stars still move you forward. If a level does not click, go and play the daily puzzle and come back later. Very often the solution is obvious with fresh eyes.
