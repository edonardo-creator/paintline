How to play Paintline

Slide the ball, paint every tile, and find the shortest path. Here is everything you need to know, from your first swipe to the trickiest tiles.

The basic rule

Every level is a small board of floor tiles surrounded by walls. Your paint ball sits on one of the tiles. Swipe up, down, left or right (or use the arrow keys on a keyboard) and the ball starts rolling in that direction. It does not stop after one tile: it keeps sliding until something blocks it, usually a wall or the edge of the board.

Every tile the ball rolls over is painted in the colour of the current world. The level is solved the moment the last unpainted tile is covered. That is the whole idea, and it is surprisingly deep: because the ball always travels as far as it can, you cannot simply walk to the tile you want. You have to plan routes that end exactly where you need them to.

Moves, targets and stars

Each swipe counts as one move. Every level has a target: the length of its shortest possible solution, calculated by a solver that tries every route. Finish in the target number of moves and you earn three stars. A few extra moves still solve the level, but for fewer stars.

Levels also have a move limit. If you run out of moves before the board is fully painted, simply try again. Sometimes the game notices early that the remaining moves can no longer finish the board and tells you, so you do not waste time.

  • Undo takes back your last move. You have a limited number per attempt; the button shows how many are left. Restarting the level refills them.
  • Hint (the light bulb) shows the next best move. It is there when you are stuck, but an attempt that uses a hint can earn at most two stars.
  • Restart puts the board back to the start at any time.

Special tiles

New ideas arrive world by world. Each one is introduced by a short, hands-on guide the first time you meet it, and a friendly character explains how it works. Here is a quick reference.

●

Stoppers

A stopper is a soft dot on the floor. When the ball rolls onto it, it stops right there instead of sliding to the wall. Stoppers let you end a move in the middle of an open corridor.

➜

Arrows

An arrow tile turns the ball. Whatever direction the ball came from, it leaves the arrow in the direction the arrow points and keeps sliding.

◎

Portals

Portals come in matching pairs, circle with circle and diamond with diamond. A ball that enters one comes out of its twin and keeps rolling in the same direction.

❄

Cracked ice

Cracked ice can be crossed only once. As soon as the ball leaves it, the ice breaks and that square becomes a hole you can never enter again. Rifts in the ice carry the ball underneath a wall to the other side.

⇄

Switches and barriers

Touching a switch flips every barrier on the board: closed barriers open and open ones close. A raised, striped block is a closed barrier.

⚡

Lightning and gears

A lightning tile launches the ball over the walls in front of it. A gear carries it through a wall to the tile behind.

◇

Crystal mirrors

A mirror reflects the ball by ninety degrees, to the left or to the right depending on which side it arrives from.

♨

Geysers

A geyser throws the ball into the air. It flies three tiles forward, straight over any walls in between, and lands to keep sliding.

◷

Clock dials

A dial turns the ball in the direction its hand points. Each time the ball passes, the hand moves a quarter turn clockwise, so the same dial sends the ball somewhere else next time.

≋

Wind gates

A wind gate lets the ball through only when it travels in the same direction as the wind. Coming from any other side, the gate behaves like a wall and stops the ball in front of it.

Game modes

  • Story: travel from world to world with Damla, the smallest drop of paint, and bring colour back to the land.
  • Daily puzzle: one new puzzle every day for everybody, with a streak for coming back.
  • Time attack: solve as many boards as you can against the clock, with combo multipliers for quick, clean solutions.
  • Weekly tournament: everyone plays the same five levels; the fewest total moves wins.
  • Challenge a friend: send a level as a link and compare move counts.
  • Master difficulty: unlocked after finishing a world. The move limit equals the shortest solution, with no undo and no hints.

Controls and accessibility

Paintline is built for one thumb. Swipe anywhere on the board, or turn on on-screen direction buttons in the settings. On a computer, the arrow keys and WASD work too. Patterned tiles help players who find some colours hard to tell apart, and you can also choose larger text, reduced motion, light or dark themes and full screen reader support.
